<section id="the-list">

## Everyone has the same list

Buy a resale villa or apartment in North Cyprus and within a week you will have written the same list every other buyer writes: repaint throughout, new bathroom, probably a new kitchen, do something about the floors, maybe take down that wall between the kitchen and the living room.

It is a good list. The mistake almost nobody avoids is treating it as a shopping list rather than a **sequence**. In renovation, the order you do things in decides whether you pay for each job once or twice.

This guide sets out the order that works, what to check before any trades are booked, and how the process runs if you spend most of the year in the UK.

</section>

<section id="why-order">

## Why the order matters more than the list

The logic of renovation is simple and unforgiving: **messy work before clean work, wet problems before dry finishes, things inside the walls before the walls themselves.**

Get it wrong and the costs compound quietly. The freshly painted wall gets chased open for new wiring. The new laminate floor is fitted, then scratched under a plasterer's ladder. The bathroom is tiled, then half undone because the pipework behind it was forty years old and nobody looked.

Every one of those is the same failure: a finishing job done before the job underneath it.

</section>

<section id="survey-first">

## Before anything: survey the property

A resale property here has usually stood through twenty or more Cyprus summers and winters. Before booking any work, the property needs a proper look — not for what is ugly, but for what is wrong underneath.

<section id="damp-first">

## Water and damp come first, every time

If the survey turns up water — a leaking flat roof, a terrace that drips into the room below every winter, damp climbing a ground-floor wall, black mould in a bedroom corner — that goes to the top of the list, ahead of everything you actually wanted to do.

The reason is not caution, it is arithmetic. Water destroys finishes. Paint, plaster, laminate, fitted furniture: moisture ruins all of them, and it does not care that they are new. Redecorate over an unfixed leak and the stain is back within a few months, now in the middle of paint you have just paid for.

<InfoBox type="warning" title="Damp has more than one cause — and more than one fix">
Condensation mould, a roof leak and rising damp look similar on the wall and need entirely different repairs. Diagnosing which one you have is the first job — our article on [damp and mould in North Cyprus homes](/en/blog/damp-and-mould-in-north-cyprus-homes) explains how to tell them apart, and our [waterproofing service](/en/services/waterproofing) covers the repairs.
</InfoBox>

Only when the property is dry does the renovation proper begin.

<section id="outside">

## The outside: render, insulation and paint

Renovation budgets here have a habit of stopping at the front door. That is worth resisting, for two practical reasons.

First, **the outside protects the inside.** Cracked render takes in winter rain, and that moisture surfaces later as damp patches on your freshly decorated walls. If the facade needs repair, it belongs in the same plan as the interior.

Second, **scaffolding goes up once.** If the render is being repaired anyway, that is the moment to consider [external wall insulation](/en/blog/external-wall-insulation-north-cyprus) — the system that cuts summer air conditioning bills and winter condensation, finished with a rendered coat in the colour of your choice. Facade repairs, insulation and exterior painting are covered under our [facades and rendering service](/en/services/facades-and-rendering).

## Having the work done while you are in the UK

A large share of the renovations we carry out are for owners who are off the island for most of the year, and the process is built around that:

1. **Free survey.** We inspect the property and send you what we find, with photographs.
2. **Written quotation.** Scope, materials and programme set out in writing, in English, before anything starts.
3. **The work, documented.** Stages are photographed as they happen so you can follow progress from home.
4. **Handover.** Snagging closed out, the property cleaned and ready for you or your tenants.

<section id="timing">

## Timing it through the year

Internal work runs happily year-round. The external stages are the ones with a season:

**Waterproofing, rendering and exterior painting** want dry surfaces and moderate temperatures — spring and autumn are the reliable windows. Winter rain interrupts the work, and August heat makes renders and coatings cure too fast for their own good.

So the efficient pattern for a property with outside works is: survey and quotation over winter, external works in spring, internal fit-out following straight on — and the property ready before the summer season, whether that is for your own use or for rental.

</section>
